Before you withdraw from your RRSP

Your participation in the program begins when you meet the eligibility conditions and you make a withdrawal from your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP).

Confirm you are eligible to participate

Before making a withdrawal from your RRSP under the HBP, make sure you are eligible.
For more information: Eligibility for the HBP

Withdraw up to $60,000

You can withdraw up to $60,000 from your own RRSP under the HBP. This may be done in more than one withdrawal, but the total cannot exceed $60,000. For more information about multiple withdrawals, review Making more than one withdrawal.

You will not be taxed on HBP withdrawals that are within the $60,000 limit. However, you must report any withdrawal amounts that are above $60,000 as income on your income tax and benefit return for the year you received it. Your RRSP issuer will also have to withhold tax on the amount in excess at the time of the withdrawal.

Normally you will not be allowed to withdraw amounts from a locked-in RRSP or a group RRSP.

If you have previously participated in the HBP, refer to If you have participated before.

How withdrawing can impact your RRSP

The 89-day RRSP deduction limit

If you participate in the HBP, you may not be able to deduct all or part of your RRSP contributions made during the 89-day period before you made the HBP withdrawal.

You cannot claim a deduction for any amount you contributed during the 89-day period that is more than the RRSP's value after your made the withdrawal.

The same rules apply if you contributed to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP during the 89-day period before they made an HBP withdrawal from the same RRSP.

For contributions made to an RRSP in the 89-day period to be fully deductible, the value of that RRSP after the HBP withdrawal must be at least equal to those contributions.

Calculate the non-deductible part of a contribution

To determine the non-deductible part of an RRSP contribution made in the 89-day period before the first HBP withdrawal, you can do the following calculation. You must do a separate calculation for each withdrawal that you or your spouse or common-law partner made.

If you are the only one who contributed to your RRSP

 
 
 
Amounts you contributed during 89-day period
Do not include

Do not include the following amounts:

  • Any amounts for which you did not receive an RRSP receipt
  • Contributions that represent lump-sum amounts (for example, retiring allowances) that you transferred to this RRSP
    • However, you must include lump-sum amounts that represent contributions you made to another RRSP during the 89-day period just before your withdrawal, and that were transferred to the RRSP in question
  • The excess amount that you withdrew from your RRSPs in connection with the certification of a provisional past service pension adjustment that you re-contributed to this RRSP in the 89-day period just before your withdrawal, and for which you claim or will claim a deduction
  • An amount you contributed to this RRSP that was refunded to you as an unused amount (if you have filled out Form T3012A, Tax Deduction Waiver on the Refund of your Unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP Contributions from your RRSP, PRPP or SPP)
  • Amounts you contributed as a repayment or cancellation payment to your RRSP under the Lifelong Learning Plan

Do not include

Do not include the following amounts:

  • Any amounts for which you or your spouse or common-law partner did not receive an RRSP receipt
  • Contributions that represent lump-sum amounts (for example, retiring allowances) that your spouse or common-law partner transferred to this RRSP
    • However, you must include lump-sum amounts that represent contributions that your spouse or common-law partner made to another RRSP during the 89-day period just before your spouse's or common-law partner's withdrawal, and that he or she transferred to the RRSP in question
  • The excess amount that your spouse or common-law partner withdrew from their RRSPs in connection with the certification of a provisional past service pension adjustment, that your spouse or common-law partner re-contributed to this RRSP in the 89-day period just before their withdrawal, and for which your spouse or common-law partner claims or will claim a deduction
  • An amount you or your spouse or common-law partner contributed to this RRSP that was refunded to you or your spouse or common-law partner as an unused amount (if you or your spouse or common-law partner have completed Form T3012A, Tax Deduction Waiver on the Refund of your Unused RRSP, PRPP, or SPP Contributions from your RRSP, PRPP or SPP)

The order of the contributions has an impact

If you and your spouse or common-law partner made contributions to their RRSP during the 89-day period just before they made an HBP withdrawal, it is the earliest contributions made during this period that will be non-deductible.

How to make an HBP withdrawal

Fill out and submit the required forms

You must fill out sections in two different forms. The CRA may contact you for more information about your withdrawal.

  1. Ask your RRSP issuer to withdraw an amount from your RRSP under the HBP (you must complete this step for each eligible withdrawal you make)
    1. Fill out Area 1 (Parts A, B, C) of Form T1036, Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) Request to Withdraw Funds from an RRSP
    2. Submit Form T1036 to your RRSP issuer for them to fill out Area 2
  2. Report the withdrawals to the CRA when you file your taxes:
    1. Fill out Part E of Schedule 7, RRSP, PRPP, and SPP Contributions and Transfers, and HBP and LLP Activities (for all)
    2. Attach Schedule 7 to your income tax and benefit return for the year you made the first withdrawal
    File a tax return for every year you participate

    Starting in the year of your first HBP withdrawal, you must fill out and send us an income tax and benefit return every year until you have repaid all of your withdrawals or included them in your income.

    You must file a tax return even if you have declared bankruptcy or do not owe any tax.

Making more than one withdrawal

You can make multiple withdrawals for up to a total of $60,000 from your RRSPs under the HBP, but there are restrictions. You are only allowed to make multiple withdrawals in the same calendar year as your first withdrawal, and in January of the following calendar year.

You cannot make withdrawals outside this period unless expressly permitted by the Minister.
