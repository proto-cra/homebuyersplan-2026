When you can cancel

If you met the HBP eligibility conditions and made an RRSP withdrawal under the HBP, you generally cannot cancel your participation.

However, you may be able to cancel it if one of the following situations apply.

You did not buy or build

You did not buy or build a qualifying home or replacement property.

Cancellation payment due date

If you are cancelling your participation under the HBP because you did not buy or build a qualifying home or replacement property, your cancellation payments are generally due by December 31 of the year after the year you made your first HBP withdrawal. However, if you obtained a one-year extension but still did not buy or build a qualifying home or replacement property, your cancellation payments are due by December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first HBP withdrawal.

You divorce or separate

You may be able to cancel your participation if you made a withdrawal under the HBP following the breakdown of a marriage or common-law partnership if both of the following apply:

  • you did not dispose of an owner-occupied home at the time of the withdrawal before the end of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first withdrawal under the HBP
  • you did not acquire your spouse or common-law partner's interest or right in the owner-occupied home 
Cancellation payment due date

If you cancel your participation under the HBP following the breakdown of your marriage or common-law partnership for the reasons outlined above, your cancellation payments are due by December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first withdrawal under the HBP.

You became non-resident

You may be able to cancel your participation if you became a non-resident before buying or building a qualifying home or a replacement property.

If you become a non-resident after a qualifying home is bought or built, you cannot cancel your participation in the HBP. However, special rules will apply to the repayment of your HBP balance. For more information, go to HBP participant becomes a non-resident.

Cancellation payment due date

If you did not maintain your Canadian residency until you were required to acquire or build a qualifying home or replacement property, your cancellation payments are generally due by December 31 of the calendar year after the calendar year you made your first withdrawal under the HBP (if you obtained a one-year extension to acquire or build a qualifying home or replacement home, this due date will be extended to December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first HBP withdrawal.) However, if you are a non-resident of Canada when you file your income tax and benefit return for the year in which you made the HBP withdrawal, the due date for the cancellation payment is the earlier of the following dates:

  • December 31 of the calendar year after the calendar year you made your first withdrawal under the HBP
  • The date you filed your income tax and benefit return for the taxation year you made your first withdrawal

You're helping a specified disabled person

You may also be able to cancel your participation if you made a withdrawal under the HBP to help a specified disabled person acquire a home, and one of the following situations apply:

  • that person does not buy or build a qualifying home or replacement property
  • you become a non-resident before that person buys or builds a qualifying home or a replacement property

Cancellation payment due dates

You did not buy or build

Your cancellation payments are generally due by December 31 of the year after the year you made your first HBP withdrawal.

If you obtained a one-year extension

If you obtained a one-year extension but still did not buy or build a qualifying home or replacement property, your cancellation payments are due by December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first HBP withdrawal.

You divorce or separate

Your cancellation payments are due by December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first withdrawal under the HBP.

You became non-resident

Your cancellation payments are generally due by December 31 of the calendar year after the calendar year you made your first withdrawal under the HBP.

If you obtained a one-year extension

If you obtained a one-year extension to buy or build a qualifying home or replacement home, your cancellation payments are due by December 31 of the second calendar year after the calendar year of your first HBP withdrawal.

If you filed your income taxes as a non-resident for the year of your first withdrawal

If you are a non-resident of Canada when you file your income tax and benefit return for the year of your first withdrawal, the due date for the cancellation payment is the earlier of the following dates:

  • December 31 of the calendar year after the calendar year you made your first withdrawal under the HBP
  • The date you filed your income tax and benefit return for the taxation year you made your first withdrawal

How to cancel

If you choose to cancel and repay the full amount you withdrew under the HBP to your RRSPs by the required due date, your withdrawal will not be taxed. Any portion of your withdrawal that is not repaid by the required due date will have to be included in your income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return for the year you made the withdrawals.

You can make your cancellation payments to any of your existing RRSPs, or a new RRSP for yourself. A cancellation payment cannot be made to your pooled registered pension plan (PRPP) or specified pension plan (SPP), or to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSPs or SPP.

A cancellation payment is not considered to be an RRSP contribution and must not be entered as a contribution in "Part A – Contributions" on Schedule 7. You cannot claim a deduction for this amount on your income tax and benefit return.

To cancel your participation, do the following:

  1. Make a cancellation payment to any of your existing RRSPs, or a new RRSP for yourself by the due date.
  2. Send a completed Form RC471, Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) – Cancellation or a letter explaining the reason for your cancellation to the CRA. The completed form or the cancellation letter must be received by the CRA no later than 60 days after the date your cancellation payments are due. The cancellation letter must include all of the following information:
    • the reason why your participation in the HBP is being cancelled
    • your last name, first name, complete address, and social insurance number (SIN)
    • your signature
  3. Attach your cancellation payment receipts for any repayments to your letter or to a filled out Form RC471.
  4. Send the completed Form RC471, Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) – Cancellation or the letter to the CRA including your receipts. This information can be submitted by mail or by using your CRA account. If you would like to submit this information by mail, send it to one of the addresses below.

If your residential address is in Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, Nunavut, Northwest Territories or in the following cities in the province of Quebec (Montréal, Québec City, Laval, Sherbrooke, Gatineau and Longueuil), send your request to:

Canada Revenue Agency

If you can't cancel

If you are not able to cancel your participation in the HBP, you will have continue to make your minimum annual repayments until the balance is paid in full.

If you are not able to repay the minimum annual amount, you must include the remaining balance as income on your income tax return.

For more information: If you are not able to repay.
