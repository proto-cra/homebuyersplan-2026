Make an HBP repayment

You can repay the withdrawals you made under the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) by making a contribution to your registered retirement savings plan (RRSP), pooled registered pension plan (PRPP) or specified pension plan (SPP) and designating it as an HBP repayment. (You cannot repay your HBP withdrawals into your FHSA.)

  1. Contribute to your RRSPs, PRPPs, or SPP in the year the repayment is due or in the first 60 days of the year after
  2. Complete Part B of Schedule 7, RRSP, PRPP and SPP Contributions and Transfers, and HBP and LLP Activities and enter the amount of the repayment on line 24600 to designate it as an HBP repayment
  3. Report the repayment to the CRA by attaching Schedule 7 to your income tax and benefit return when you file your taxes that year
What to know about filing your income taxes when you participate in the HBP

You must fill out and send us an income tax and benefit return every year until you have repaid all of your withdrawals or included them in your income.

You must file a tax return even if you have declared bankruptcy or do not owe any tax.

When you fill out your tax return:

  • Do not report your repayment amounts on line 12900 of your tax return
  • Do not include any RRSP contributions designated as an HBP repayment on line 20800 of your tax return
  • You cannot claim an amount you designated as a repayment as a deduction on your tax return

When a contribution can't repay HBP

Not all contributions you make to your RRSPs, PRPPs, or SPP in the repayment year or in the first 60 days of the year after can be designated as an HBP repayment/p>

You cannot designate contributions that:

  • you make to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSPs or SPP (or that they make to your RRSP)
  • you transfer directly to your RRSPs, PRPPs, or SPP from a registered pension plan (RPP), deferred profit sharing plan (DPSP), registered retirement income fund (RRIF), first home savings account (FHSA), SPP, or another RRSP or PRPP
  • you withdrew from one of your RRSPs, PRPPs or SPP to have a provisional past service pension adjustment approved and that you re-contributed to this RRSP, PRPP or SPP and for which you can claim a deduction
  • you designate as a repayment under the Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) for the year
  • you made in the first 60 days of the repayment year, that you already deducted on your income tax and benefit return for the previous year
  • you already designated as a repayment for the previous year under the HBP or the LLP
  • you receive in the repayment year (such as retiring allowances) that you transfer to your RRSPs, PRPP or SPP and deduct or will deduct on your income tax and benefit return for that year

For other situations, refer to: Life events that affect repaying the HBP

How repaying can impact your RRSP

Repaying does not affect your RRSP deduction limit. You can still contribute to your RRSPs, PRPPs or SPP and designate those amounts as HBP repayments, even if your RRSP deduction limit is zero.

We consider these amounts to be HBP repayments and not RRSP contributions. As a result, you cannot claim a deduction for these amounts on your income tax and benefit return.

For more information about the impact on RRSPs, refer to: How withdrawing can impact your RRSP
