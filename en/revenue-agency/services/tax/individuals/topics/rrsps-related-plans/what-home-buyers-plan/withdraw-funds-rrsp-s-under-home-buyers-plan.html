Before you withdraw from your RRSP

Your participation begins when you meet the eligibility conditions and make a withdrawal from your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP).

Check you are eligible for the HBP

Before applying to make a withdrawal under the HBP, refer to Eligibility for the HBP to make sure you are eligible.

How much you can withdraw under the HBP

You cannot withdraw more than $60,000 in total under the HBP. If you have previously participated in the HBP, refer to If you have participated before.

Any portion of the withdrawals from your RRSPs under the HBP that exceed $60,000 will have to be reported as income on your income tax and benefit return for the year you received it. In addition, your RRSP issuer will have to withhold tax on the amount in excess at the time of the withdrawal.

  • You cannot withdraw more than $60,000 in total from your RRSPs under the HBP (in each participation period). You are only permitted to make withdrawals under the HBP from your RRSPs.
    • Your RRSP issuer will not withhold tax on the amounts withdrawn under the HBP where the total amounts withdrawn do not exceed $60,000.
    • Normally you will not be allowed to withdraw amounts from a locked-in RRSP or a group RRSP.

How withdrawing can impact your RRSP

The 90-day RRSP deduction limit

If you made contributions to your RRSPs during the 89-day period before you withdrew the amount for your HBP withdrawal, your RRSP contribution may not be deductible.

If you participate in the HBP, certain rules limit the deduction of your RRSP contributions made during the 89-day period before you made a withdrawal under the HBP. Under these rules, you may not be able to deduct part or all of the RRSP contributions made during this period.

You cannot deduct the amount by which the total of your contributions during the 89-day period to your RRSP is more than the fair market value of that RRSP after the withdrawal.

The same rules apply if you contributed to your spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP during the 89-day period before that individual made the withdrawal from the same RRSP under the HBP.

In other words, for contributions made to an RRSP in the 89-day period to be fully deductible, the value of that RRSP after you made a withdrawal under the HBP must be at least equal to those contributions.

How to make an HBP withdrawal

Fill out and submit the withdrawal form

You must fill out the form for each eligible withdrawal. The CRA may contact you to ask for more information about your withdrawal.

  1. Fill out Area 1 of Form T1036, Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) Request to Withdraw Funds from an RRSP
    1. You must meet all the conditions in Area 1
  2. Submit the form to your RRSP issuer for them to fill out Area 2
  3. Report the withdrawal at tax time of the year of your first withdrawal:
    1. Fill out Part E of Schedule 7, RRSP, PRPP, and SPP Contributions and Transfers, and HBP and LLP Activities (for all)
    2. Attach Schedule 7 to your income tax and benefit return
    File your income taxes for every year you participate

    Starting in the year of your first HBP withdrawal, you must fill out and send us an income tax and benefit return every year until you have repaid all of your withdrawals or included them in your income.

    You must file a tax return even if you have declared bankruptcy or do not owe any tax.

Making more than one withdrawal

You can make multiple withdrawals from your RRSPs under the HBP, but there are restrictions. You are only allowed to make multiple withdrawals in the same calendar year as your first withdrawal, and in January of the following calendar year.

You cannot make withdrawals outside this period unless expressly permitted by the Minister.
