When to start repaying

You have up to 15 years to repay the total amount you withdrew from your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP).

You repay your HBP balance  in annual repayments to your RRSPs, pooled registered pension plan (PRPP) or specified pension plan (SPP) until you have repaid the total amount you withdrew under the HBP.

The start date of your 15-year repayment period depends on when you make your first HBP withdrawal from your RRSPs.

If you made your first withdrawal:

Before January 1, 2022

Your repayment period started the second year after the year you made your first HBP withdrawal.

For example, if you made your first withdrawal in 2020, your first year of repayment was 2022.

Between January 1, 2022, and December 31, 2028

Temporary repayment relief is available to defer the start of the 15-year repayment period by an additional 3 years.

This means your repayment period starts the fifth year after the year your made your first HBP withdrawal.

For example, if you made your first withdrawal in 2026, your first year of repayment is 2031.

You may also repay the full amount into your RRSPs, PRPPs, or SPP at any time.

If you start repaying early

If you choose to start your repayments earlier, your repayment period will stay the same. Any repayments made before you are required to start your repayments will reduce the amount you have to repay for the first year.

If the early repayments are more than the required amount for the first repayment year, the difference will reduce your HBP balance and the minimum required annual repayment amounts over the remaining repayment period.

How much to repay

Find your annual repayment amount

Your minimum annual repayment amount is included in your HBP statement  on your most recent notice or assessment (NOA) or reassessment (NOR).

In some cases, we may send your HBP statement on Form T1028, Your RRSP, HBP, LLP and FHSA Information.

Your HBP statement takes into account any additional repayments you made and gives you the minimum required repayment you must pay for the next year.

Repaying more than the minimum

If you repay more than the minimum required annual repayment for a particular year, your minimum required repayments for later years will be reduced.

You will still have to make repayments in the following years until the remaining HBP balance  is zero.

To calculate the minimum required repayment you have to repay for the next year, divide your HBP balance by the number of years remaining in your repayment period:

Temporary repayment relief does not apply

In 2019, Suzanne withdrew $16,500 from her RRSP to participate in the HBP. Her minimum required repayment starting in 2021 was $1,100 ($16,500 ÷ 15).

Suzanne made repayments of $1,100 for 2021, 2022 and 2023.

In 2024, she contributed $8,000 to her RRSP and designated that amount as a repayment under the HBP for 2024.

If you are not able to repay

Repaying less than the minimum amount

If in a given year you repay less than the minimum required annual repayment indicated in your HBP statement , you must include the difference between the HBP repayment you made and the minimum required repayment for that year as RRSP income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return.

Do not include in your RRSP income an amount that is more than the minimum required repayment for the year minus the amount you designated as an HBP repayment.

Repaying none of the minimum amount

If you do not make the minimum required repayment to your RRSPs, PRPPs, or SPP, you must include it as RRSP income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return.

The amount you include on line 12900 is the minimum required repayment as shown on your HBP statement of account. Your HBP balance  will be reduced accordingly.
