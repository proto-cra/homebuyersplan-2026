If you divorce or separate

If there is a breakdown of the marriage or common-law-partnership, each partner must continue to repay their Home Buyers’ Plan (HBP) withdrawals to their respective RRSP, PRPP or SPP. Even if you move, sell your home or buy a new one, your repayment schedules stay the same.

In some situations, you may be able to cancel your participation in the HBP if you meet the cancellation criteria.

For more information: Cancelling the HBP

If you become non-resident

You must be a resident of Canada to participate in the HBP. If you become non-resident, you can no longer participate in the program. This directly impacts your ability to designate RRSP contributions as repayments under the HBP.

Non-resident before you buy or build

If you become non-resident after you make your HBP withdrawals but before you buy or build a qualifying home, you must do one of the following:

  • Cancel your participation in the HBP and make your cancellation payment
  • Include your remaining HBP balance as RRSP income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return for the year you made the withdrawals

For more information: Cancelling the HBP

Non-resident after you buy or build

If you become non-resident after you buy or build a qualifying home, you must do one of the following:

  • Repay your remaining HBP balance to your RRSPs, PRPPs or SPP by the earliest of the following dates:
    • Before the time you file your income tax and benefit return for the year that you become a non-resident
    • 60 days after you become a non-resident
  • Include your remaining HBP balance as RRSP income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return for the year that you become a non-resident

If you reach the age of 71

You can no longer contribute to an RRSP, PRPP, or SPP after the end of the year in which you turn 71 years of age. As a result, you will no longer be able to repay any amounts you withdrew from your RRSPs.

In the year you turn 71, do one of the following:

Repay the full amount
Repay the remaining HBP balance to your RRSPS, PRPPs or SPP.
Repay a partial amount
Make a partial repayment to your RRSPs, RPPs or SPP. Your remaining HBP balance at the beginning of the year you turn 72 will be divided by the number of years remaining in your repayment period.
That calculated amount must be included as income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return for each remaining year.
Make no repayment
Make no repayment to your RRSPs, PRPPs or SPP. Your HBP balance at the beginning of the year you turn 71 will be divided by the number of years remaining in your repayment period.
That amount must be included as income on line 12900 of your income tax and benefit return for each remaining year.

If the participant dies

If a participant in the HBP dies, the remaining HBP balance can be included either as part of their income or transferred to a spouse or common-law-partner.

Include balance as income

If the participant of the HBP dies, the general rule is that their legal representative must include the participant's remaining HBP balance in the participant's income for the year of death.

The amount to be included is equal to the participant's HBP balance before death less any RRSP contributions (made before the participant died) that were designated as an HBP repayment for the year of death.

Transfer balance to spouse

At the time of death, if the participant had a spouse or common-law partner who is a resident of Canada, that individual can jointly elect with the participant's legal representative to make the repayments under the HBP. By doing so, the income inclusion rule will not apply for the participant.

The participant's HBP balance at the time of death less any HBP repayments made for the year of death is treated as if the surviving spouse or common-law partner withdrew it, and it has to be repaid to that individual's RRSPs.

If the spouse does not have their own HBP

Generally, if the surviving spouse or common-law partner was not participating in their own HBP and elects to continue making the repayments of the deceased individual, the surviving spouse or common-law partner would be considered a participant in an HBP and cannot make any withdrawals under the HBP until the balance is completely repaid and all the other applicable HBP conditions are met.

The deceased participant's HBP balance must be repaid over the same number of years remaining in the participation period of the deceased participant.

If the deceased participant had not made an HBP repayment for the year of death, and the election is made, the minimum required repayment for that year for the deceased's participation would not be required. The surviving spouse or common-law partner would continue to make the minimum required repayments for the remaining years.

If spouse has their own HBP

Before the participant dies, the surviving spouse or common-law partner may have become a participant in their own HBP. There are no negative tax consequences for the surviving spouse or common-law partner if, as a result of electing to treat the deceased participant's HBP balance as their own, their new HBP balance exceeds their HBP withdrawal limit.

At the time of death, if the surviving spouse or common-law partner is also a participant of their own HBP and the election described above is made, their revised HBP balance must be repaid over the remaining number of years of their own repayment period.

How to transfer the HBP:

Option A

  1. The surviving spouse or common-law partner and the deceased participant's legal representative must either:

    • Fill out Form RC98, Election to transfer the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) or Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) balance at time of death

    • Write and sign a letter explaining that an election is being made to have the surviving spouse or common-law partner continue making repayments under the deceased participant's HBP, and to not have the income inclusion rule apply for the deceased participant

  2. Attach the completed form or the signed letter to the deceased participant's final income tax and benefit return

Option B

  1. The surviving spouse or common-law partner and the deceased participant's legal representative must do one of the following:
    • Fill out Form RC98, Election to transfer the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) or Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) balance at time of death
    • Write and sign a letter explaining that an election is being made to have the surviving spouse or common-law partner continue making repayments under the deceased participant's HBP, and to not have the income inclusion rule apply for the deceased participant
  2. Attach the completed form or the signed letter to the deceased participant's final income tax and benefit return
